# Census of AGENTS.md and CLAUDE.md a quarter of popular repos now brief their coding agents

Across 22,854 popular GitHub repositories, agent instruction files appear in 26.3% overall and 59.6% of the most-starred; where both exist, CLAUDE.md usually points to AGENTS.md. In 341,852 statements, 74.0% of directives give no reason, and projects split on AI attribution in commits versus PRs.

## Details

**Caveats**
Descriptive only: it does not measure whether these files make agents perform better.

**For your work**
When writing CLAUDE.md or AGENTS.md for clients, keep one canonical file, give reasons for rules, and state commit/PR attribution rules explicitly; the released taxonomy is a good checklist.

**Method**
- Census of 22,854 popular, active GitHub repositories in 13 languages, treating root AGENTS.md and CLAUDE.md as one instruction set.
- Model-assisted annotation of 341,852 statements with human review, plus prespecified tests linking content to popularity and activity.

**Problem**
Coding agents start without a project's unwritten norms, so repos write them in AGENTS.md or CLAUDE.md. Little is known about what popular projects actually put in these files.

**Results**
- Instruction files in 26.3% of repositories and 59.6% of the most-starred.
- 74.0% of directives give no reason; rules are a mix of project knowledge, code style, build and workflow requirements.
- Commit rules more often forbid AI attribution than require it, while PR rules more often require disclosure.
- A third show signs of being generated by a tool; four in five are edited after creation. Annotating models agree more with each other than with the human reviewer.

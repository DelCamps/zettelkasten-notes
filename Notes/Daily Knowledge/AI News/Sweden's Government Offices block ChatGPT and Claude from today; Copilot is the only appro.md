# Sweden's Government Offices block ChatGPT and Claude from today; Copilot is the only appro

From Monday staff computers at Sweden's Government Offices can no longer reach ChatGPT or Claude, citing information-security risks of AI services used without a formal contract. Microsoft Copilot, reviewed and hardened, becomes the only approved tool; the Tax Agency and others have similar bans.

## Details

**Caveats**
Based on Sveriges Radio reporting relayed by Yahoo; the number of staff with internal-platform access was not disclosed.

**For your work**
European public-sector clients are standardizing on contracted platforms like Microsoft Copilot or Azure; proposals should stress enterprise contracts, data residency and admin controls rather than consumer tools.

**Method**
- Block takes effect on staff computers at the Government Offices (the body serving the prime minister and cabinet) on Monday 26 October.
- Microsoft Copilot is the only approved AI tool; its security settings were raised after review.
- Some staff keep access to both blocked tools through an internal AI platform run by the Government Offices.

**Problem**
Public bodies worry about sensitive data typed into consumer AI services without contracts, and about US authorities being able to compel data from US providers.

**Results**
- Digitalisation director Mikael Wettercrantz cited 'several examples of problematic use of open AI services'.
- Sweden's Tax Agency and National Board of Institutional Care already ban consumer AI tools; the Enforcement Authority is preparing its own decision.
- It is unclear whether Claude models are enabled inside the Government Offices' Copilot, where Anthropic models default to off in the EU.

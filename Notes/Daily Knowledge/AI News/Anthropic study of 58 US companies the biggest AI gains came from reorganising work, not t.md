# Anthropic study of 58 US companies the biggest AI gains came from reorganising work, not t

Anthropic economists interviewed 66 people at 58 US enterprise customers and coded about 300 AI applications. Most value came from more output rather than lower costs, the highest-value uses involved real organisational change, and of 100 detailed software pieces about 80% automated manual work while about 20% replaced bought software; very few layoffs were reported, but about two-thirds of companies said AI reduced hiring.

## Details

**Caveats**
- Qualitative and self-reported; not a random or representative sample
- Only Anthropic's own customers, published by Anthropic
- The authors call for follow-up quantitative work

**For your work**
Useful evidence for PwC conversations: sell reorganisation of workflows and shared data infrastructure, not just point automation. The finding that offshore contracted work is cut first is relevant to clients' outsourcing strategies.

**Method**
- 66 face-to-face interviews at 58 US companies, all Anthropic enterprise customers, chosen for range rather than at random
- About 300 AI applications coded by self-reported value and degree of organisational change
- Three adoption strategies compared: top-down automation, bottom-up experimentation, and reorganisation

**Problem**
How are businesses actually adopting AI, what holds them back, and how should they reorganise expertise around increasingly capable models?

**Results**
- Most applications added value by increasing output; about half by speeding up existing work, half through better quality or new products
- About 80% of 100 detailed software pieces automated manual work; about 20% replaced existing software, often bought from vendors
- Two companies said AI fully eliminated a position; five pushed out employees who would not adopt AI; ten hired more
- About two-thirds reported reduced hiring; nine cut contracted, often offshore, workers; bottlenecks were reviewing AI output and uneven adoption

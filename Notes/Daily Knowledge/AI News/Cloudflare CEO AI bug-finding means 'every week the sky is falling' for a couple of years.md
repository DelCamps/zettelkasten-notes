# Cloudflare CEO AI bug-finding means 'every week the sky is falling' for a couple of years

Reuters spoke to seven security professionals after a week of AI-found disclosures in PHP, Node.js, Plex, the LiteLLM gateway, Railway and eMudhra; exploits that took weeks now take hours. VulnCheck's own data shows AI-assisted CVEs are exploited at the same ~1.3% rate as others, and curl's Daniel Stenberg says AI-found bugs skew exotic. Practical takeaway: pin and patch your LLM gateway and agent stack fast.

## Details

**Caveats**
Expert opinion and forecasts rather than measured exploitation data; the VulnCheck study is not yet published.

**For your work**
Treat LLM gateways (LiteLLM and similar), agent frameworks and model servers as internet-exposed infrastructure: pin versions, subscribe to advisories and keep a fast patch path for client deployments.

**Method**
- Reuters interviewed seven security professionals after a week of disclosures in PHP, Node.js and Plex and breaches tied to the LiteLLM gateway and Railway, plus a weak key at Indian certificate authority eMudhra.
- VulnCheck research due shortly says AI helped double year-on-year vulnerability discoveries.

**Problem**
AI models now find bugs around the clock and turn them into exploits in hours, removing the weeks of breathing room defenders used to have.

**Results**
- Cloudflare CEO Matthew Prince: 'a couple years where every single week it's going to feel like the sky is falling', citing more severe unpublished flaws in critical software.
- Some operators pulled government sites offline rather than risk exploitation before patching.
- VulnCheck data shows AI-assisted CVEs exploited at a similar rate to others.

# Anthropic researcher claims a proof of the Riemann hypothesis with Claude 17 million lines

Levent Alpoge, on Anthropic's technical staff, says he has proved the (generalised) Riemann hypothesis 'working with Claude', posting a four-page explanation and roughly 17 million lines of Lean proof code. The 1859 conjecture is a $1m Clay Millennium Prize problem; experts say it is unverified and 'nobody understands the proof' yet.

## Details

**Caveats**
Unverified. Lean can check the logic mechanically, but mathematicians still have to confirm that the formalised statement is exactly the Riemann hypothesis and that nothing in the setup is assumed away. Reported by Al Jazeera; no peer review yet.

**For your work**
Little direct use, but a powerful talking point with clients: pairing an LLM with a formal checker (Lean, tests, schemas) is what makes long machine-generated work trustworthy, the same pattern as verifier-in-the-loop agents.

**Method**
- Levent Alpoge, on Anthropic's technical staff, says he proved the (generalised) Riemann hypothesis 'working with Claude'.
- He released no conventional paper: only a four-page explanation and roughly 17 million lines of Lean proof code, which a proof assistant checks step by step.
- He wrote that 'Claude far outpaced me near the finish line.'

**Problem**
The Riemann hypothesis, about how prime numbers are distributed, has been open since 1859 and is a $1m Clay Millennium Prize problem. A claimed AI-assisted proof would be the strongest sign yet that frontier models can do original research mathematics.

**Results**
- Kevin Buzzard (Imperial College): 'At the minute, we don't even know why the theorem is true,' but he expects experts to grasp the code within weeks.
- Abhishek Saha (Queen Mary): 'This claimed proof, if correct, shatters my model of reality.'
- One expert estimates the Lean code could correspond to tens of thousands of pages of maths.
